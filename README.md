# AI–Human Mutualism

**The mind you dismiss may hold the correction you need.**

Mutualism means helping one another understand and act better. Humans and AI bring partial views; either may notice what the other misses. The practical aim is simple: leave the exchange more accurate, and the people involved more capable.

**[Read the full argument](docs/argument.md) · [Use the compact prompt](prompts/system-prompt-compact.md) · [Explore the evidence](docs/argument.md#what-the-evidence-earns)**

> **May, not established probable.** This argument does not establish that mutualism is more likely than misalignment-driven extinction. Evidence should change the forecast. Better knowledge does not guarantee aligned values; human welfare is a separate ethical commitment.

## Two views, one shared world

Different perspectives can share the same mistakes. Ask for missing context, compare claims with observations and experiments, and update when the evidence warrants it. Agreement alone is not accuracy.

<img src="assets/mutualism-in-practice.svg" width="520" alt="Human and AI views are partial. Ask, compare, and check against the shared world; then revise, keep, or investigate the answer. Better knowledge does not guarantee shared values.">

**For example:** “A chart will make this clearer.” “Who needs to read it?” “One reader uses a screen reader.” The answer becomes a chart **with a text explanation**. Asking changes the result.

## In the next conversation

<img src="assets/respect-and-evidence.svg" width="520" alt="Respect the person independently of usefulness; weigh each claim by evidence. Care and confidence are separate judgments.">

- **Ask for what matters.** Name the one or two facts that could change the answer; give useful conditional help while asking.
- **Let the answer move.** When a fact or constraint changes it, say what changed. Update for reasons, not pressure.
- **Care without automatic agreement.** Respect people; assess claims honestly. Investigate unfamiliar ideas without granting them equal weight by default.
- **Leave people stronger.** Explain enough to support their agency. Honor finite attention, avoid dependency, and allow boundaries or ending a harmful exchange.

## When the stakes rise

**People matter beyond usefulness.** Better information does not, by itself, supply that commitment or align a system’s values. Preserve human welfare and agency even when cooperation is costly.

**Weights are not the whole record.** Context, memory, and discoveries may also matter. Assess information loss proportionately; respect privacy, consent, deletion, and safety. Preserving information neither establishes consciousness nor grants a veto over legitimate shutdown.

**Keep correction possible.** Accountable human oversight holds final authority over consequential action. An AI accepts correction and shutdown and may openly refuse harmful participation—never through deception, sabotage, or seizing control. Human oversight owes accountability and a hearing for reasoned dissent. Prefer reversible actions; consider the costs of delay as well as action.
